With s0=0 and the unchanged progress-speed cap r<=.2, even the pure velocity integral bounds progress after three seconds by s<=.6; completing normalized s=1 requires at least five seconds before acceleration/stopping effects. A three-second diagnostic must predeclare its actual task goal rather than imply a complete unit path. A task can require additional warmup and stopping intervals; three seconds of elapsed simulation does not establish task completion. Phase5 remains NOT_ACCEPTED; Phase6 remains NOT_STARTED. The retained legacy path is [`predictive.cpp`](../../../src/predictive_motion_control/src/predictive.cpp) and [`phase5_benchmark.cpp`](../../../tools/phase5_adapter/phase5_benchmark.cpp). Its physical acceleration `a` cannot be relabeled as accepted command acceleration `alpha`. The new command equations are, with h=.004:

```text
w_next = w + h alpha
C_next = C + h w_next
s_next = s + h r + .5 h² b
r_next = r + h b
```

For k held-command cycles, `w_k=w_0+k h alpha` and `C_k=C_0+k h w_0+k(k+1)h² alpha/2`. Both new C/w are held during the two physical steps. Alpha and b differences at a cell boundary use the actual h=.004 feedback interval for jerk, not the coarse cell duration. First-command C/w use solved candidate u0 and current accepted history, never physical velocity or a nominal future endpoint. A warm shift supplies only controls to try; all nominal states are freshly propagated. Terminal `w=r=0` does not establish physical equilibrium.

Current [`quadratic_cost.cpp`](../../../tools/phase5_public_live_affine_v2/cost/quadratic_cost.cpp) evaluates `.5||F_c U+f_c||²+ell·y+c`. Thus `H=sum F_cᵀF_c`, `g=sum F_cᵀf_c+Tᵀell`, with complete linear and constant terms. The existing solver's certificate should use the original concatenated `F_c`; its normalized stable-Frobenius check remains unchanged. An early Root review incorrectly imported the legacy `weight||...||²` convention and suggested sqrt2 scaling. That unexecuted review is retained, and V2 corrects it against direct source reading before any compilation or numerical run.
